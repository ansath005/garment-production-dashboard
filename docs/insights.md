# Insights and Recommendations – Garment Production Performance Dashboard

Data: 1,197 team-days, 1 January to 11 March 2015 (UCI dataset 597).
All numbers below come directly from the dashboard.

---

## Key findings

### 1. On average the factory meets target, but more than 1 in 4 team-days miss
Average actual productivity was **73.5%** against an average target of **73.0%**. However, only **73.0%** of team-days met their target, so **27.0%** missed. The overall average looks healthy, but it hides many weak days.

### 2. Finishing has a higher average but misses target much more often
- **Finishing:** average **75.3%** (target 73.7%), but met target on only **59.7%** of 506 team-days.
- **Sewing:** average **72.2%** (target 72.5%), but met target on **82.8%** of 691 team-days.

Finishing results are very uneven. **8 of the 10 worst team-days** were in Finishing. On these days the target was mostly **80%**, but actual output was only about **24–31%**. None of them had a style change, and only one had a short stoppage (4 minutes), so **the cause of these very bad days is not recorded in the data**.

### 3. The weakest results are concentrated in four Finishing teams
Four Finishing teams met target on fewer than half of their team-days:

| Team (Finishing) | % target met | Team-days |
|---|---|---|
| Team 06 | 31.4% | 35 |
| Team 08 | 35.8% | 53 |
| Team 07 | 41.5% | 41 |
| Team 11 | 48.3% | 29 |

In Sewing, every team met target on at least **69.0%** of its days, and the best team (Sewing Team 12) reached **98.3%**.

### 4. Performance dropped in February
The share of team-days meeting target fell from **78.0% in January** to **65.9% in February**. Weekly average productivity was below target for three weeks in a row:

| Week starting | Actual | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 14 Feb | 69.2% | 72.9% |
| 21 Feb | 67.4% | 73.5% |
| 28 Feb | 69.5% | 72.0% |

It recovered to **75.0%** of team-days meeting target in March, but March has only 11 days of data.

### 5. Stoppages and style changes are linked to lower productivity in Sewing
**Stoppages (Sewing only):**

| Group | Avg actual | % target met | Team-days |
|---|---|---|---|
| No stoppage | 72.8% | 84.4% | 673 |
| Had stoppage | 49.2% | 22.2% | 18 |

**Style changes (Sewing only):**

| Group | Avg actual | Avg target | % target met | Team-days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 changes | 74.8% | 74.0% | 85.7% | 544 |
| 1 change | 61.5% | 66.2% | 69.3% | 114 |
| 2 changes | 66.2% | 68.8% | 81.8% | 33 |

Managers already set **lower targets** on style change days (66–69% instead of 74%). Even so, days with one change averaged about **4.7 points below** their reduced target.

**Not main drivers:** average productivity changed very little by day of the week (**72.3%** to **75.2%**), and the trend line for overtime per worker is almost flat.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Focus on Finishing Teams 06, 07, 08 and 11.** Review their daily results with team leaders and check whether the 80% target is realistic for them. Learn from the best teams (for example Sewing Team 12 at 98.3%) and share their working methods.

2. **Record the reason for every low-output day and every stoppage.** The worst Finishing days have no stoppage or style change in the data, and there were only 18 stoppage days in Sewing. Simple daily reason codes (machine breakdown, no material, absent workers, quality rework) would show which problem to fix first.

3. **Plan style changes better.** Group similar styles together and prepare materials and machines for the next style before the change (the Lean method **SMED**, quick changeover). Lower targets on change days are not enough on their own, because teams still miss them.

---

## Limitations

- The data covers only **1 Jan – 11 Mar 2015**, and March is incomplete.
- Some groups are small (**18** stoppage days, **33** two-change days), so these results are less certain.
- The charts show **association, not cause**. For example, lower productivity on stoppage days does not prove the stoppage caused all of the loss.
- Team numbers repeat across departments, so teams were compared within each department.
- The dataset is from one garment factory in Bangladesh, so results may not apply directly to other factories.
